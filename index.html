Reference Helper

Fill in what you can see on the book, article or page. You get the in-text citation and the reference-list entry, in the Harvard style from the STADIO Referencing Guide 2026 (Cite Them Right, 13th edition).

Unofficial helper. It follows the guide's formulas, but the guide, your lecturer and your assessment brief come first. Check every reference against the source itself before you hand in.

Start from a link, DOI or ISBN

Paste a web link, a YouTube link, a DOI (journal articles) or an ISBN (books). The tool reads the real details from the source and fills in the form below. It never guesses or invents anything.

What can it read, and what can't it?

Journal articles (DOI) and books (ISBN) work best. A YouTube link gives the title and the channel, but you must type the year it was uploaded. For web pages it depends on the site: many show the title, author and date, some block automatic reading or hide the date. Edition and page numbers are never found automatically, so you add them. To read a web page the tool asks a free public relay service to open it for you, so do not paste links that contain passwords or personal details.

How the list must look in your assignment
  • Heading Reference list: on the last page, bold, not underlined, left aligned, with a colon, one font size larger than your text.
  • Single line spacing, left aligned, one blank line between entries.
  • Alphabetical by author. Every source appears once, however often you cite it.
  • Every in-text citation needs an entry, and the first two items (author, year) must match.
  • Pictures: use a separate image reference list after it (see the guide, section 3.7).
